Watch it work

The real plugin on screen, one feature at a time: one vocal and two bars of a beat become a new track, and every sound you hear is played on Splinter.

From a sample to a beat

Splinter is built around the way producers flip samples: chop it, play it, shape it, then let Flip suggest a pattern.

Chops on the hits

Drop a sample onto the window and Splinter reads its tempo and key from the file name, or works them out by listening. It cuts wherever a new sound starts and puts each chop on its own pad. Prefer equal slices, or want to place the cuts yourself? Switch to Even, or to Tap and tap a pad as it plays.

16 pads, each its own sound

Every pad has its own pitch, start and end, attack and release, filter, resonance and drive, and can play in reverse, so one sample becomes a whole kit, or a melody.

Eras

Give any pad the sound of a classic hardware sampler: crunchy 12-bit, the darker, rounder top end of a 26 kHz sampler, or worn and dusty like an old record. Or keep it clean.

Flip

One button turns your chops into a new pattern that plays in time with your project. Flip again for another idea, step back to one you liked, lock the rows you want to keep, then drag the MIDI onto a track.

Swing and note repeat

Hold a pad and it repeats in time: 1/8, 1/16, 1/16 triplets or 1/32, following your project's tempo. Swing pushes every other 16th late, from straight to a heavy shuffle.

Plays from your gear

The pads answer to any MIDI pad controller or keyboard your DAW can see, with velocity, so you can finger-drum your chops straight away. Or click them.

Flip: new patterns from your chops

Stuck on a loop? Flip writes a pattern from your chops so you have something to react to, and the result is ordinary MIDI that you can edit like any other.

  1. Chop

    Load a sample. The chops land on the hits, one to a pad, and Splinter works out which chops sound like kicks, snares and hats.

  2. Flip

    Choose 1, 2 or 4 bars and how busy the pattern should be, then press Flip. Kick-like chops land on the strong beats and snare-like ones on the backbeat, with your swing.

  3. Keep what works

    Lock a row you like and it stays exactly as it is when you flip again. Step back and forward through every flip you've made.

  4. Drag it out

    Drag the pattern from Splinter onto a track as a MIDI clip, at your project's tempo, then move notes, change velocities or chop it up further.

How do I install it?

Unzip the download and copy Splinter.vst3 into your VST3 folder: C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3 on Windows or ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3 on a Mac. Logic users also copy Splinter.component into ~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/Components. Then rescan plugins in your DAW and load Splinter on an instrument (MIDI) track. The zip includes these steps in INSTALL.txt.

How many computers can I use it on?

All the computers you personally own or use. See the licence agreement for the details.

Can I use it on commercial releases?

Yes. The music you make with Splinter, including the patterns Flip writes, is entirely yours, with no royalties or credits owed. You still need the rights to the samples you load into it.

Do updates cost anything?

No. Updates to Splinter version 1 are free for everyone who bought it.

What if it doesn't work on my system?

Get in touch within 14 days of buying. If we can't get it working, you get a full refund. See the refund policy.

Does it work in Pro Tools?

Not yet. Pro Tools only loads AAX plugins, and Splinter is VST3 and AU.

Will it work with my MIDI controller?

Yes. The 16 pads sit on MIDI notes C1 to D#2, where most pad controllers start, and they respond to velocity. Any controller or keyboard your DAW can see will play them, and you can click the pads with a mouse too.

Does it come with sounds?

No. Splinter is made for flipping your own samples: drag in any WAV, AIFF, FLAC or Ogg file up to 60 seconds long, or click the Kit button to pick one.

Do I need to keep my sample file?

No. Splinter saves the sample inside your DAW session, along with its chops, your pad settings and your flips, so the session opens the same on another computer.
