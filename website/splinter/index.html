From a sample to a beat

Splinter is built around the way producers flip samples: chop it, play it, change the pattern, then give it some character.

Chops on the hits

Drop in a loop and Splinter finds its tempo and key, cuts it where the hits land and puts each chop on its own pad. Prefer even slices, or want to tap them in yourself? Switch to Even or Tap.

Flip

One button turns your chops into a new pattern, in time with your project. Flip again for another idea, step back to one you liked, then drag the MIDI onto a track in your DAW.

Eras

Give any pad the sound of a classic hardware sampler: crunchy 12-bit, the dark warmth of a low sample rate, or dusty and worn. Or keep it clean.

16 pads, each its own sound

Every pad has its own pitch, start and end, attack and release, filter and drive, so one sample can become a whole kit.

Swing and note repeat

Swing the pads to taste, and hold a pad with note repeat on for rolls at 1/8, 1/16, 1/16 triplets or 1/32.

Plays from your gear

The pads answer to any MIDI pad controller or keyboard your DAW can see, with velocity, so you can finger-drum your chops straight away.

Flip: new patterns from your chops

Stuck on a loop? Flip writes a pattern from your chops so you have something to react to, and the result is ordinary MIDI that you can edit like any other.

  1. Chop

    Load a sample. The chops land on the hits and the pads light up in the same colours as the waveform.

  2. Flip

    Choose 1, 2 or 4 bars and how busy the pattern should be, then press Flip. It follows your project's tempo and your swing.

  3. Keep what works

    Lock a row you like and it stays put when you flip again. Step back and forward through your last flips.

  4. Drag it out

    Drag the pattern from Splinter onto a track as MIDI, then move notes, change velocities or chop it up further.

Eras

Each pad has its own Era, so the drums can be gritty while the melody stays clean. Each one gives you the sound of a type of sampler, not a copy of any one machine.

EraWhat it sounds like
CleanYour sample exactly as it is.
12-bit gritThe crunch of early 12-bit samplers: punchy drums with a hard, gritty edge.
Vintage 26kA low sample rate that rounds off the top end and adds a dark, warm weight, like a sample from an old machine.
DustyWorn and lo-fi: softer highs, a little noise and wobble, as if the loop came off an old record.

Pad controls

Pick a pad and its controls appear next to the grid. These are the controls planned for every pad.

ControlWhat it does
PitchTunes the chop up or down in semitones.
Start / EndTrims where the chop starts and stops playing.
Attack / ReleaseFades the chop in and out, from a hard hit to a soft swell.
Filter / ResonanceTakes the top off a chop, with resonance to add a peak.
DrivePushes the chop into saturation for weight and bite.
EraClean, 12-bit grit, Vintage 26k or Dusty, per pad.
ReversePlays the chop backwards.
One-shotPlays the whole chop from one tap, however briefly you hit the pad.
ChokePads in the same choke group cut each other off, like an open and closed hi-hat.

When is Splinter out?

It's being built now. When it's ready, the download will be on this page.

How much will it cost?

The price will be announced at launch.

Will it work with my MIDI controller?

Yes. The 16 pads sit on MIDI notes C1 to D#2, where most pad controllers start, and they respond to velocity. Any controller or keyboard your DAW can see will play them, and you can click the pads with a mouse too.

Which DAWs will it run in?

Any DAW that loads VST3 or AU instruments, such as Ableton Live, FL Studio, Cubase, Reaper, Studio One, Bitwig and Logic Pro, on Windows, macOS and Linux.

Do I need my own samples?

No. It will come with factory kits to start from, and you can drop in your own samples whenever you like.

Is Flip's MIDI mine to use?

Yes. Patterns you make with Flip, and the music you make with Splinter, are entirely yours.
